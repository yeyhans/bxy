# Sources: "history of space travel, i guess" (read 2026-11-05)

Every fact in the voice and on screen, where it's used, and where it comes from.

| Fact as said / shown | Source (opened today) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| L1-L2: 1957, the Soviet Union launches the first artificial satellite, a metal ball that beeps; launched from Baikonur (Tyuratam, Kazakhstan) | [Sputnik 1 (Wikipedia, archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130228051930/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_1); [Museum of Flight: Sputnik](https://museumofflight.org/spacecraft/sputnik); [CSIRO ATNF](https://www.atnf.csiro.au/ATNF-DailyImage/archive/2019/04-Oct-2019.html) | 4 Oct 1957. Its radio beeps were picked up by radio amateurs worldwide. |
| L3: it beeps for three weeks, then the battery dies (it keeps orbiting) | same | The transmitter ran 21 days (some sources count 22) until 26 Oct 1957; the satellite stayed in orbit until 4 Jan 1958. "(still going around)" on screen. |
| L4: America gets very nervous | [Sputnik crisis (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_crisis) | A period of public fear and anxiety in the US. Some historians call the panic more elite than public, so the line says "very nervous", not "panics". |
| L5: then a stray dog from Moscow goes up, her name is Laika | [Laika (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika); [Nature: A dog's life](https://www.nature.com/articles/449541a) | Sputnik 2, 3 Nov 1957; Moscow strays were picked on purpose. Her death on the flight is not joked about or shown. |
| L6: she ends up on stamps | [Nature: A dog's life](https://www.nature.com/articles/449541a) | Mongolian and Romanian stamps among others. The stamps on screen are drawn by us; their years are decorative, not real issues. |
| L7: 1961, Yuri Gagarin says "let's go" and goes around the whole planet | [Vostok 1 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_1); [ESA: Forty years on](https://www.esa.int/About_Us/Corporate_news/Forty_years_on) | 12 Apr 1961, one orbit; "Poyekhali!" ("let's go") at liftoff. |
| L8: he's home in under two hours (clock to 1:48) | [Vostok 1 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_1) | 108 minutes from launch to landing; landed by parachute near Engels, Saratov region. |
| L9: 1969, two guys land on the Moon and plant a flag | [Apollo 11 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) | 20 Jul 1969, Armstrong and Aldrin. |
| L10: when they leave, the engine blows the flag over | [Science Focus: is the flag still on the Moon?](https://www.sciencefocus.com/space/is-the-flag-still-on-the-moon); [LROC image 537](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/images/537) | Aldrin saw it fall at liftoff; LRO images show the Apollo 11 flag casts no shadow while the others still stand. |
| L11: twelve people walk on the Moon; then, 1972, everyone stops | [Space.com: how many people have walked on the Moon](https://www.space.com/how-many-people-have-walked-on-the-moon); [Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-missions/how-many-people-walked-on-moon) | Apollo 11-17; the last walk was Apollo 17, Dec 1972. The script says nobody *walks* there after, which is still true. On screen the counter climbs two people per landing, with the year box in step: Apollo 11 and 12 (1969) make 4, Apollo 14 and 15 (1971) make 8, Apollo 16 and 17 (1972) make 12. |
| L12: 2026, four people fly all the way back and just drive past it | [Artemis II (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_II); [NASA: Artemis II lunar flyby](https://www.nasa.gov/gallery/lunar-flyby/) | Launched 1 Apr 2026, crewed lunar flyby on 6 Apr, no landing (Wiseman, Glover, Koch, Hansen). |
| L13: 2000, people move into a space station, and it's never been empty since | [Space station 15 years (TIME)](https://time.com/4094719/space-station-fifteen-years/); [phys.org: 20 years of people in orbit](https://phys.org/news/2020-11-space-station-years-people-orbit.pdf) | Continuously occupied since Expedition 1 arrived on 2 Nov 2000. |
| L14: it laps the planet about sixteen times a day; sixteen sunrises | [International Space Station (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station); [NOAA Science On a Sphere: ISS](https://sos.noaa.gov/catalog/datasets/43/) | About 15.5-16 orbits a day, so the script says "about sixteen"; one sunrise per orbit. |
| L15: 2015, a rocket comes back down and lands, standing up | [Space.com: SpaceX lands orbital rocket](https://www.space.com/31420-spacex-rocket-landing-success.html); [Landing Zones 1 and 2 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Zones_1_and_2) | 21 Dec 2015 (local time), Landing Zone 1, Cape Canaveral, Florida: the first orbital-class booster landing. No company name or logo on screen. |
| L16: now they catch them with giant chopsticks | [NPR, 13 Oct 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/10/13/nx-s1-5151788/spacex-starship-booster-caught-first-launch); [The Engineer](https://theengineer.co.uk/content/news/spacex-super-heavy-rocket-caught-by-chopsticks-arms) | Starbase, Texas: the booster caught by the tower's arms on the first try. The tower and booster are drawn generic. |
| L17: if you're under 26, there's never been a day without people in space; counter 9,499 days | the 2 Nov 2000 date above | 2 Nov 2000 to 5 Nov 2026 = 9,499 days (computed). Someone under 26 today was born after 5 Nov 2000. The counter reads "since nov 2, 2000". |

## The pictures
- **The Earth:** NASA Blue Marble Next Generation and Black Marble 2016 (NASA Earth Observatory, public domain).
- **The Moon:** NASA's CGI Moon Kit, the LRO colour map (NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio, public domain).
- **The satellite, the rockets, the station and the lander** are our own generic 3D models, with no markings or logos. The flag is a simple drawn flag with no emblem.

## Left out
- How Laika died (accounts differ, and it isn't a joke).
- "America panics" (the panic is disputed).
- The exact count of people who have been to space (it depends on where space starts).
- Company names on screen.
