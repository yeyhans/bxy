# Sources: "history of coffee, i guess" (read 2026-11-05)

Every fact in the script and on screen, where it's used, and where it comes from.

| Fact as said | Source (opened today) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| L1: coffee (Coffea arabica) grows wild in the highlands of south-west Ethiopia | [Coffea arabica (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffea_arabica); [Coffee (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) | Kaffa / Kefa plateau; the exact history of domestication is unclear, so the script only says "grows wild". |
| L1-L2: the legend of a goat that eats the berries and gets lively; first written down in 1671 (Antoine Faustus Nairon) | [Kaldi (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaldi); [Mental Floss: who discovered coffee](https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/coffee/who-discovered-coffee) | Probably apocryphal, so the script says "legend says". The herder's name is a later addition and isn't used. |
| L3: in the fourteen hundreds, Sufis in Yemen drink coffee to stay awake through night devotions | [Coffee (Wikipedia), History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee); [Muslim Heritage: the coffee route from Yemen to London](https://muslimheritage.com/coffee-route-yemen-london/) | The earliest credible evidence is the mid-15th century; earlier claims are contested and left out. |
| L4-L5: 1511, Mecca bans coffee; the sultan in Cairo overturns the ban | [Popular Science: 4 times coffee was illegal](https://www.popsci.com/health/coffee-ban-history/); [Tasting Table: 10 times coffee was banned](https://tastingtable.com/1029221/times-coffee-was-banned-around-the-world); [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2025/09/brewing-controversy-how-coffee-sparked-fierce-debate-in-the-16th-century/) | The quick reversal from Cairo is the version most sources give. How long it took, and the governor's fate, vary, so neither is said. |
| L6: 1652, London gets a coffee house (St Michael's Alley, Cornhill) | [Pasqua Rosée (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasqua_Ros%C3%A9e); [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/places/londons-original-all-inspiring-coffee-house) | Not called "the first": Oxford had one earlier (1650-51) and the founder is disputed. |
| L7: coffee is everywhere now | common knowledge | No number is claimed. |

## Left out
- The herder's name (a later invention), and the 9th-century date some retellings give the legend.
- Claims of 10th-century coffee drinking (contested readings of old texts).
- "First coffee house in England" (Oxford came first) and the founder's name.
- A cups-per-day number (the commonly quoted figures are estimates from industry groups).
