# Sources: "history of the internet, i guess" v2 (read 2026-11-04)

Every fact in the script, where it's used, and where it comes from. If sources disagree, the safer wording is used.

| Fact as said | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1969: two computers in California get connected (UCLA and SRI). UCLA tries to send "login", the system crashes after "lo"; the full login went through about an hour later. | [Leonard Kleinrock, UCLA: the first words of the Internet](https://www.lk.cs.ucla.edu/internet_first_words.html); [CBS News, 45 years ago](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/first-message-sent-over-the-internet-45-years-ago) | Oct 29, 1969, 10:30 pm. |
| 1971: Ray Tomlinson sends the first network email between two computers standing side by side. He doesn't remember what it said (probably something like QWERTYUIOP). He picked the @ sign. | [Ray Tomlinson (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Tomlinson); [Computing History UK](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/det/6116/first-e-mail-sent-by-ray-tomlinson) | His own words: the test messages were "entirely forgettable". QWERTYUIOP is his best guess, so the script says "probably". Why @: "it just makes sense" (it wasn't used in names); the script says only that he picked it. |
| Jan 1, 1983: the ARPANET switches every computer to TCP/IP on one day ("flag day"), often called the internet's birthday. About 400 computers were on it. | [Flag day (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_day_(computing)); [InternetNews, 30 years of TCP/IP](https://internetnews.com/news/30-years-of-tcp-ip-dominance-began-with-a-deadline) | "Birthday" is a popular framing, so the script says "the internet's birthday, kind of". |
| 1989: Tim Berners-Lee at CERN writes the proposal for the web; his boss Mike Sendall writes "vague but exciting" on it. | [CERN: the original proposal](https://info.cern.ch/Proposal.html); [CNBC, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/03/12/feedback-world-wide-web-inventor-berners-lee-got-from-boss-on-the-idea.html) | The CERN copy with the note survives. |
| 1991: the first website goes public, and it's a page about the web project itself. | [Web Design Museum](https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/web-design-history/tim-berners-lee-created-the-first-website-1991); [info.cern.ch](https://info.cern.ch/) | Public on Aug 6, 1991 (CERN dates the first page to Dec 1990). |
| 1991: Cambridge scientists point a camera at their coffee pot so they don't walk to an empty one. It's on the web from 1993 and runs until 2001. | [Trojan Room coffee pot (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Room_coffee_pot); [Guinness World Records: first webcam](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/114885-first-webcam) | The script says "for ten years", which matches 1991-2001. |
| 1990s dial-up: the modem makes a screeching handshake noise, and a phone call on the same line knocks you offline. | common knowledge of dial-up modems | No date on screen. |
| 1995: the first thing sold on eBay's predecessor (AuctionWeb, run from Pierre Omidyar's home in San Jose, California) is a broken laser pointer; the buyer reportedly collected broken laser pointers. | [TIME](https://time.com/4018841/first-item-sold-listed-ebay/); [Web Design Museum, eBay 1995](https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/web-design-history/ebay-1995); [eBay: Meet the Buyer of the Broken Laser Pointer](https://ebayinc.com/stories/news/meet-the-buyer-of-the-broken-laser-pointer) | The collector line comes from Omidyar's telling (TIME). **eBay's own later story says the buyer wanted a cheap one to repair**, so the script's "apparently" is doing real work here. No price on screen. |
| (not in the final script) 1998: two Stanford students start a search engine in a rented garage in Menlo Park. | [Google garage (UW PCAD)](https://pcad.lib.washington.edu/building/6973); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/the-career-rise-of-susan-wojcicki-who-rented-her-garage-to-googles-founders-in-1998-and-is-now-the-ceo-of-youtube/amp_articleshow/67315727.cms) | No company name or logo on screen. |
| 2000: the dot-com bubble bursts. | common history (NASDAQ peak March 2000) | |
| 2005: the first video on the biggest video site is 19 seconds of a guy at the zoo talking about elephants' trunks. It's still up. | ["Me at the zoo" (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_at_the_zoo); [10News San Diego](https://www.10news.com/news/local-news/san-diego-news/1st-ever-youtube-video-was-taken-at-the-san-diego-zoo-posted-20-years-ago-today) | Apr 23, 2005, 19 s. |
| 2020s: computers you can talk to; one wrote and drew this video. | our own build: Claude wrote the script and every frame is code | True of this video. |

**Left out on purpose:** the founders' names (Page and Brin, Omidyar, Karim) and every company name and logo. They aren't needed for a joke, and logos are banned in our media.
